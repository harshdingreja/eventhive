One council, dozens of clubs, one pass.

EventHive is how the Council of Organizations runs registrations and entry for every club event: no paper forms, no queue at the desk, just a QR code on your phone.

About COG

The Council of Organizations brings the college's technical, cultural and sports clubs under one roof. It plans the semester calendar so that events don't clash, shares venues fairly between clubs, and looks after the student experience from the first announcement to the last check-in.

Before EventHive, each club ran its own Google Form and printed its own attendee list. Popular events were overbooked, lists went missing, and the entry desk had no way to tell a real registration from a screenshot. Now every event has a hard seat limit and every pass works exactly once.

Experiment 1 also asks for superscripts and subscripts: this is the 3rd semester the council has used digital passes, and the canteen's free H2O refill station sits right next to the registration desk.

How to get your pass

  1. Create a student account with your college email.
  2. Browse upcoming events and filter by category, date or open seats.
  3. Press Register. If a seat is free, your pass appears instantly.
  4. At the venue, show the QR code. A volunteer scans it and you are in.

What the council's admins can do

  • Publish, edit and delete events, with a seat limit for each.
  • See every participant and search by name, email or registration ID.
  • Check students in by camera or by typing the ID, with a log of every scan.
  • Follow registrations and turnout on a live dashboard.

Questions students ask

Can I register a friend?
No. Every pass belongs to one account, and the volunteer at the gate checks the name on it.
The event says "Full". Is there a waiting list?
Not yet. Seats free up only if the organizers raise the capacity, so check back before the event.
What if my phone dies?
Tell the volunteer your registration ID (it looks like EH-7K3F-9Q2A), or print the pass beforehand.
Can a screenshot of someone else's pass get me in?
Only once, and only if it hasn't been used yet; the second scan is rejected and logged.
